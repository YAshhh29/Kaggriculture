# Sprint journal — two new agents for the 2800–3000 band

Started 2026-09-26. Updated as work happens: what was done, what was measured,
what went wrong, and what it means. Numbers are measured unless marked
otherwise.

## The goal

Two agents, built in parallel, aimed at a live rating of 2800–3000:

1. **Clone-plus.** Study the top 500 teams — several replays each, wins and
   losses — take the strongest approach available as a base, and put our own
   demand/market layer on top so it can beat the thing it was built from.
2. **Bespoke.** Built from what the top 500 and the public code teach, so that
   it wins on its own terms.

Plus: a fresh corpus, an arena where games can actually be watched, rigorous
testing before anything is submitted, and this journal.

### Why earlier sessions stalled

Read from the history rather than guessed:

- **Tape panels overstate strength.** Recorded opponents cannot react; when our
  selling moves prices their recorded buys fail and they collapse. Agent D won
  108/184 against top-500 tapes and held ~1744 live. K won 48% against bracket
  tapes and holds ~1467 live.
- **The live benchmark was stale.** strong_panel's "strong" opponents were
  aurax7 v7 and ahmedberatozer v34 — early-September public code — plus our own
  H2 at 2265. None is in today's 2600–2900 band.
- **The public field moved and nobody looked.** There are now public notebooks
  claiming 2945–2965 (see below). None had been downloaded.
- **Measurements were contaminated.** A fetch writing into the tape directory
  while a panel read it changed which games each arm played.

### 4. What the public field really is

- **One lineage.** Tschinkel 2945, metav4 v13, haideptry "2965", guru master
  v3/v4, leoprovorov forecast and tetsutani demand all embed the identical block
  of code (an "E182 Shop0909 last-seven-turn closure planner", a `_V219_REPORT`
  table) at nearly the same line. They are forks of one stack: Ahmed Berat
  Ozer's V39/V40 chassis over yhay81's Shop Router tapes, plus layers from
  prvsiyan, Dmitrii Gluzdov, aurax7, tetsutani and others. A large share of the
  2800–2950 opponents are very likely running variants of it.
- **The 2945 Farm, from its own code:** replays one of 41 recorded route
  tapes, picked on day 6 from the first two town shops, with ~25 "reflex"
  layers on top. The layers mostly change *what is sold when*: pull planned
  sales forward while the book is above base, sell before predicted rival
  sales (matching against 1,998 recorded rival sale streams), order the ten
  market slots by exposure to the rival's batch, recover exactly what the
  rival sold each turn from public data, sell what the midnight shed drop
  would destroy.
- **Its author's negative results match ours.** Holding premium goods back
  for a better price lost $7.5k–$34k a game, and so did selling less — every
  sale also denies the rival the book. Same finding as K2's lot cap and shed
  drain.
- **It goes 0–36 against seven top-10 teams** (its own claim). The best public
  code loses every game to the real top 10. Cloning it can place us near the
  lineage; it cannot, by itself, reach the top.

### 5. Why a 2% edge is worth 1,400 rating points

The 2945 Farm beats K 6 out of 6 — by only 1,100 to 4,900 coins each time.
Ratings come from *win probability*, not margin. An agent that wins almost
every game by 2% is rated far above its opponent. This is the real target:
**consistent small wins against the lineage**, not bigger farms.

### 6. Calibration — predicting live ratings locally

Identical code rates lower the later it is submitted (H2's exact file: 2252.6
on Sept 16, 2133.3 on Sept 17, 1576.2 on Sept 21), so old ratings describe an
easier ladder. Six of our recent submissions were matched to their exact files
by byte count and serve as anchors: J 685, C2 1050, F 1344, I 1381, K 1467,
H2 1576. `tools/arena/calibrate.py` fits a strength for every agent from local
games, maps strength to rating with the anchors, and checks itself by
predicting each anchor with that anchor left out.

**Bug caught before it poisoned this:** F and C2 end in a one-argument
`decide(observation)`. Kaggle calls agents with only as many arguments as they
declare; the arena's timing wrapper always passed two, every turn raised, the
farms stood still at exactly 3,000 coins — and the engine still reported the
games as DONE. Fixed, and the arena now records every exception an agent
raises and marks that game, so silent failure can't pass as a result again.

### 15. The ceiling on route-based agents — measured by three independent studies

Extraction agents read the analysis notebooks and **re-checked their claims on
our own corpus**:

**destbreso, "x-ray your agent":**
- *"The strongest agents compose their plan per game. They do not replay a
  tape and they do not branch off a fixed trunk."* Top 30: **26 of 26 are live
  schedulers** (day 6–11 plans repeat 0–12% game to game). Ranks 101+: mostly
  "branchers", i.e. route replayers.
- *"Copies of one public lineage crowd the ladder below 2700. Above 2700
  opponents are original, and no mirror game occurs."* Median plan agreement:
  0.504 at 2600–2699, **0.006–0.011 above 2700**.
- **The day of the second land purchase is the strongest single separator by
  rating** (Spearman −0.70): day 9 at 2700+, day 11 below.
- Stronger teams CARE less (r = −0.45; the lineage over-cares at ~400) and
  never sell into a floored book (r = −0.45).

**georgymamarin, "what 2600 farms do differently"** (confirmed on our corpus):
- Table stakes at every band 2400–3100: land day 6, 12-hand crew, ~33
  strawberry, 8 cows, 5–6 sheep.
- The separating crop moves with the meta: tomato marked 2800+ on Sept 22–23,
  had spread to 2700–2800 by Sept 25; the current 2800+ marker is a **third
  land quadrant plus more carrot**.
- Paired within-game edges (seats within 50 rating points): **≥260 plantings
  wins 66%** (n=283), ≥10 cows 63%, ≥50 carrot 62%, ≥290 hires 60%, ≥3
  quadrants 59%.
- Games are decided by ~3% of the bank (median winning margin 2,787 coins).

**raykkretzschmar, "findings from zero to top meta":**
- Rating plateaus come from losing a few close games to top agents, not from
  low margins against everyone else.
- Opening feed denial is real: an earlier-slot wheat buy at turn 0 leaves a
  later five-wheat buy able to afford only four, and an animal starves.
- Among near-clones, sell timing and slot order decide games; one-turn
  pre-emption on wheat and fertilizer flips near-mirror matchups.

**What this means:** anything built on replayed routes — Agent A included —
competes in a pool of its own copies and tops out around 2700–2800. It can
beat those copies (tetsutani does, 98%) and climb to the top of that pool,
but 2800–3000 means playing like the top 30: a **live scheduler** that plans
each game from its own state. J is one; its problem is execution, and every
part of it now has a numeric target from the top of the ladder.

**A trap avoided.** The floor-sale marker looked like a clean layer to build.
But the engine says sales at $1 do not increase market supply: a floor sale
earns a coin and does not worsen the book. Weak farms make them because they
over-produce into dead books; gating the sale would only strand stock. A
statistic about a stronger agent is not an instruction — the same lesson as
last session's shed drain. Of these markers, only production capacity (land
on day 9, more plantings) plausibly causes wins, and a route agent cannot
absorb it without rewriting its routes.

Also: tetsutani's build contains no PREDICT layer (that is Tschinkel's v9/4),
so rebuilding PREDICT's stale library would improve a weaker variant, not ours.

### 23. Agent 1, attempt 1: an SE wheat/carrot project — built, and it lost

`rl/se_project.py` wraps a complete agent with a fourth-quadrant project. The
parent gets a **masked observation** (SE shown locked; the project's hands,
seeds and unsold stock removed), so none of its ~40 layers can see or disturb
the project; the project hires after the parent, keeps its own seed ledger
(the engine cancels *every* PLANT of a crop in a turn that asks for more
seeds than exist), and sells only what its own hands delivered.

The masking worked: the parent's orders were identical to Agent A's until day
19. The project did not: **Agent 1 57,030 vs Agent A 91,633** (seed 11).

Two reasons, both found by measurement:

1. **Labour.** Hands are dismissed every night and re-hired at fib(n) for the
   n-th hire of the day, so above an 11-hand crew a project hand costs 144,
   then 233, then 377 *per day*. At ~10 tiles per hand and ~$37 per tile-day,
   one extra hand barely pays; the project got ~1 hand a day, and SE went to
   weeds. The top 30 can use SE because their 11 hands are efficient enough to
   cover four quadrants; the land is not the edge, the labour efficiency is.
2. **It killed V219.** Agent A's own day-18 tomato project needs SE locked. In
   this town V219 sold ~80 tomatoes at up to **$390 each**. Taking SE first
   threw that away — that is almost the whole 34k.

### 29. Agent L's first market layer: queue order

The engine settles orders slot by slot, trading both players' i-th orders one
unit at a time at a shared price, so a sale in an earlier slot than the
rival's same-item sale gets the undamaged price and the rival sells into the
damage. Copies of A put their orders in the same slots every time.
`rl/market_front.py` reorders the parent's own orders (never quantities):
orders that don't trade against the market go behind every SELL/BUY_PRODUCT,
and among those the order is chosen to maximise the price damage avoided
against a copy of ourselves, using the engine's exact price formula (checked:
0 mismatches).

| test | result |
|---|---|
| L_front vs A, 30 seeds × 2 seats (baseline: all draws) | **54 / 60 won**, median +154; the 3 losing seeds lose by 17–45 |
| vs the 6 strongest public agents + H2, 4 seeds × 2 seats | same wins as A (44/48) but a larger margin against **every** opponent (+200 to +400 a game) |
| 2600–2700 recordings (faithful) | 21/70 vs A's 19/70; median −651 vs −878 |

### 34. Rule book, robustness, and the second workflow

Read the engine's own `README.md`/`AGENTS.md` (they match the code). Value
per tile-day at base price: wheat 20, carrot 26, tomato 20, strawberry 29,
melon 137, goose 50 (100 with care), cow 80, sheep 67 — premium books crash
to $1 on gluts, animals eat wheat.

Robustness, from `kaggle_environments/core.py`: one uncaught exception =
ERROR = forfeit; a malformed action = INVALID = forfeit; turns over 1 s draw
on a 60 s game reserve (TIMEOUT when empty). `rl/safety.py` is the outermost
guard: catches everything (without re-calling the parent, whose layers reset
on a repeated step), repairs the action's shape, exposes a time budget for
optional layers, and prints a `HEALTH` line at the end of the game into
Kaggle's agent logs. Wrapped around L it plays identically (4/4 draws, 0
repairs, slowest turn 184 ms, ~5 s of agent time per game).

A second workflow (`l2-live-brain`) runs alongside the first: an economics
model of the rule book (`docs/KAGGRICULTURE_ECONOMICS.md`) and three live
layers — labour (recycle silent no-ops and PASS turns with useful in-place
actions, without moving any unit), animals, and land.
